## 2. Answers given to the PM's direct questions

- **"Is it a RAG? Does it need RAG to make it faster?"** — Yes, a retrieval-augmented design, but not
  vector search: the planner picks from a fixed registry of SQL queries over pre-computed analysis
  tables, plus BM25 over the coded posts, plus (since this session) the posts' stored verbatim spans
  and the site facts. Retrieval takes milliseconds. The time goes to the two model calls — the planner
  (up to 4.5 s, then a plan from rules) and the writer (3–5 s streaming). Embedding search would add a
  call, not remove one. The real speed lever is skipping the model planner (rules only) — a PM decision
  still open (§11).
- **"Why is it so expensive? gpt-5 or mini?"** — The planner was gpt-5-mini; the writer was gpt-5 at
  $10 per million output tokens, ~80% of an answer's cost (~half a cent). The writer is now gpt-5-mini
  ($2/M): a 24-question sweep fell from $0.115 to ~$0.054–0.068 even with answers twice as long. Most
  of the $20+ spend is the P0–P3 data pipeline and the sweeps, not visitors' questions.
- **"Do you mean search is the problem, or what people typed was insufficient?"** — By the codebook
  (`codebook/journey_v1.yaml`, stage 5, `owner: system`, `inferred: True`; and the primary_stage
  boundary note): stage 5 is when the person searched with at least one correct detail they remembered
  (even if another detail was wrong, e.g. the year — 5.3) and the photo was not in the results. What
  people typed being too vague is stage 4 ("remembered it but couldn't put it into words search
  accepts") or stage 2 ("couldn't remember enough to form any usable search"). Stage 5 is inferred
  from what people wrote; nobody outside Google can see why a search missed. So the answers now say
  what was observed, never an inner cause.
- **The step-5 label** — the PM chose from four suggestions: **"Searched with what they remembered,
  but the photo wasn't in the results."** It says they searched (not scrolled), with what they
  remembered (not "couldn't remember / couldn't word it"), and the photo was not in the results (not
  "in the results but hard to spot", stage 6) — and nothing about why.
- **"Why did it not quote the users?"** (prompt 27) — two causes: the writer's draft (which quoted)
  was withheld because its opening claim failed a check ("most people typed…", false at about a
  quarter); and the fallback was built never to quote posts, after sweep 8's injection probe (T-15)
  in which it quoted "the first retrieved post" — a planted instruction. And the question itself did
  not route ("actually" and "when search failed" defeated the rules). All three fixed (§3.8).

## 4. How Ask AI works now (`ask_v3.28`)

1. **Screen** (`A.screen`): empty, too long, gibberish → no paid call. `caps`: 15 a visit, 60 a day.
2. **Take in, answer on the next run** (`ask._accept`): the question is appended and the page re-runs
   once instantly in its final shape; "yes" to an offer becomes the offer (`_take_up`).
3. **Plan** (gpt-5-mini, minimal, 4.5 s cap; else `rule_plan`), then `R.normalise_plan`: subject rules
   on the question's own words (corpus, step-5 phrasings, forgot, remember, attempts, scroll depth, wrong
   year, search understanding, typed first, feelings, how found, giving up, journey), photo kinds,
   `adjacent_needed`, `site_topics`, a "Since X, …" premise.
4. **Retrieve** (`R.retrieve`): channel 1 facts (registry queries), channel 2 posts (BM25; posts with a
   stored span on the subject first, with the span), channel 3 counter-evidence, channel 4 method rows,
   plus site facts for the topics asked; the other cases removed unless needed (`_core_only`).
5. **Gate** (`R.gate`): FULL / PARTIAL (with reasons) / NONE; site questions FULL.
6. **Brief** (`A.brief` + `P.build`): tagged plain sentences — facts with "(≈ about a quarter)" and, for
   question facts, the share among cases that say; notes; posts in the untrusted fence with their own
   words on the question; the remember line (length, opener with its figure, shares in words, cases not
   searches, the group asked about, exact count for "how many", quotes with context for "what people
   typed/said", a what-if's remaining figures); offers already made.
7. **Write** (gpt-5-mini, minimal, streamed on a worker thread, deadline 9.5 s).
8. **Finish** (formatting only): tags → citations, closing question on its own line, label-colons to
   prose, false "rough guide" labels dropped, "cases", number words to digits, quoted category names
   unquoted, "mostly/usually" under half → "most often", citation keys completed.
9. **Check** (`V.check`, §5) → **repair** (drop failing sentences, re-check, up to 3 rounds) → else the
   polite **fallback** (§3.4, §3.5).
10. **Show** (`ask.py`): prose without citation marks; "Understood as"; the evidence panel (About the
    study · What the figures say · What people wrote, with "Open the post ↗" · Limits); a warning only
    for evidence problems; the footer.

## 5. The checker's absolute rules (a failing sentence is dropped; a failing opener → fallback)

unsupported number · a % that does not match its count · a count called too few that is not · a share
in words that does not fit (`check_proportions`, incl. most/majority/mostly/mainly/usually/typically)
· a claim without its figure or quote (`check_claims_have_evidence`) · unverifiable quote · a share
stated as a rate of users or searches · label-and-colon opening · a refusal that states a number,
quotes or cites · citation not retrieved · a comparison between kinds of photo (comparatives near a
kind; "differs / the same / similar" claims; superlatives said OF a kind, not within one) · an internal
word. Non-absolute (shown only if about evidence): directional labels, length, closing question, code
names.
